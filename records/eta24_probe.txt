eta24 probe — reach probabilities and estimator dispersion (2026-09-16)
=====================================================================

Machine: M2 Pro, 12 threads, on AC, not idle (load average 2.6-3.8). Times are
thread-time sums except "wall".

The instrument
--------------
`eta24 probe` (src/bin/eta24.rs) walks k steps from GOAL (LongMoveDfa window 14,
moribund on, prefix checkpoints every 8), accepts boards proven at distance k
(zpdb k6), and for each accepted board v computes the reach probability P(v)
(src/puzzle24/eta/reach.rs): a backward search from v bounded at k marks the
interval of boards on length-k paths, and a forward pass over (board, walker
node) pushes probability from GOAL through it. Per depth it reports:

  stage A           walk + rejection search, per attempt
  stage B           reach probability, per accepted board
  interval          boards on some length-k path between GOAL and v
  P(v)/walk         reach probability over the probability of the walk taken
                    (1 when only one allowed path reaches v)
  |V_k|, eta_k      Horvitz-Thompson: mean over attempts of 1/P(v) and of
                    w(blank) b^-MD(v) / P(v) (zero for rejected attempts),
                    eta_k divided by 25!/2; 95% intervals from the per-attempt
                    sample variance
  ESS, largest      Kish effective sample size of the nonzero terms, and the
                    largest single term's share of the sum

Correctness checks behind it (unit tests, src/puzzle24/eta/reach.rs):
- P(v) equals the summed probability of exhaustively enumerated walks for
  every board at k = 7, 9, 11, moribund on and off (relative error < 1e-12).
- Horvitz-Thompson over 6,000 sampled walks recovers |V_12| = 24,098 and the
  exact sum of b^-MD over V_12 (from BFS layers) within four standard errors.

Reading
-------
1. The estimators are consistent where references are exact or tight: |V_k|
   within 1 SE of A090031 at k = 12, 20, 30 and of the thesis at 45, 56.
2. The 1/P(v) weights are heavy-tailed. The effective sample size of |V_k| is
   6% of accepted boards at k = 30, 0.7% at 45, 0.6% at 56; single boards
   carry 10-32% of the sum from k = 45 up. P(v) varies through the number of
   allowed paths to v (median 5, up to ~500 at k = 64) and through the product
   of per-step choice counts along them.
3. eta_k adds a second heavy tail: within a sphere, the few boards with low
   Manhattan distance dominate b^-MD. ESS of eta_k is at most 98 of 4,000 even
   at k = 12, where every P(v)/walk ratio is 1.
4. With ESS in single digits the normal intervals and the projected attempt
   counts understate the error. A heavy right tail makes typical sample means
   fall below the true mean. The k = 64 estimates (|V_64| 2.2 SE below the
   thesis; eta_64 an order of magnitude below the ~2.5e-21 per-sphere average
   implied by the exact total of ~1e-19 spread over spheres 30-64) look like
   that.
5. Stage B is ~20% of thread time per accepted board at k = 64 (355 ms against
   ~1.4 s of stage A per accepted board). Its cost is the backward search
   (1.7M nodes mean, 32M max) proving which boards lie on length-k paths; the
   interval itself is small (560 boards mean).

Result 3 — where the weight dispersion comes from
-------------------------------------------------
`eta24 probe --diagnose --k 30,45,56 --attempts 40000` (same walker, same
seed, 43 s wall). Each accepted weight W = 1/P(v) factors as
(1/walk probability) / M: the choice product of the walk taken (the product
of allowed-move counts along it) over the multiplicity M = P(v) / walk
probability (other allowed paths to v).

Reading
-------
1. The choice product causes the degeneracy. Holding M fixed changes ESS
   little (2062 vs 1667, 91 vs 62, 29 vs 9); holding the walk probability
   fixed leaves ESS near the sample count. log W tracks the number of 3-way
   steps (correlation +0.77 to +0.86): a walk that meets more 3-way choices is
   less probable per path, so its endpoint is undersampled and its weight
   large. The heaviest samples took 8-11 more 3-way steps than the median.
2. The end blank's position class does not concentrate the weight, so
   stratifying by blank class would not help.
3. eta_k has a separate, larger tail: the 2-4% of accepted boards with the
   lowest Manhattan distance carry 57-80% of the eta terms while holding about
   1% of the weight. Fixing the choice-product dispersion leaves this tail.

Result 4 — one-step lookahead move choice
-----------------------------------------
Walker Choice::Lookahead (src/puzzle24/eta/walker.rs): each allowed move is
taken with probability proportional to the number of moves allowed after it
(with one fewer step left). Reach probabilities use the same weights. Tests:
every board at distance k <= 13 stays reachable, P(v) matches exhaustive
enumeration (k = 7, 9, 11), and Horvitz-Thompson recovers the exact V_12 size
and MD sum under both choices. Same seed and attempts as Results 2-3. Load
average 3.4-5.9 during these runs (higher than for Results 2-3), so compare
node counts rather than milliseconds.

Reading
-------
1. Lookahead removes most of the choice-product dispersion: its log10 spread
   halves, and the effective sample size of |V_k| rises 8x (k = 30) to 36x
   (k = 45). |V_30| now matches A090031 to 0.02% at ±1.4%.
2. The walks pass more 3-way branch points (+2.5 to +4 per walk), which end in
   boards that are more expensive to verify: 1.3-1.7x the rejection-search nodes
   per attempt and 1.3-2.6x the backward-search nodes per accepted board.
   Precision per node still improves: relative variance falls 11x at k = 56
   and 38x at k = 45, against at most 1.8x more nodes. At k = 64 the gain is
   small (ESS 45 of 222).
3. Multiplicity M now limits the size weights about as much as the choice
   product at k = 56 (ESS if only M varied 736, if only 1/walk varied 491).
4. The eta_k tail is unchanged: the lowest-MD 2-5% of accepted boards carry
   70-85% of the eta terms, and ESS of eta terms stays 3-59.

Result 5 — tilting walks toward low Manhattan distance
------------------------------------------------------
Walker::with_md_tilt(λ): on top of the lookahead weight, a move that raises
Manhattan distance is weighted b^-λ and one that lowers it b^λ. Each move
changes MD by exactly ±1 (tested), so along any path from GOAL the tilt
factors multiply to b^(-λ MD(end)) times a per-step normalisation. Reach
probabilities use the same probabilities; the enumeration, completeness and
Horvitz-Thompson tests include λ = 1. `probe --choice lookahead --md-tilt λ
--k 30,45,56 --attempts 40000`, seed 1. Load average 5.7-6.6 from the
back-to-back runs: compare yields, node counts and ESS, not milliseconds.

Reading
-------
1. The tilt reaches the eta tail at moderate depth: at k = 30, ESS of eta terms
   rises 59 -> 431 (λ = 0.5) and the interval narrows 25% -> 9%; at k = 45,
   28 -> 101 and 37% -> 20%.
2. It costs yield, increasingly with depth. Moves that lower Manhattan distance
   tend to lead back toward GOAL, so tilted walks end closer than k more often:
   at λ = 0.5 yield falls 27% at k = 30, 67% at k = 45 and 88% at k = 56. At
   k = 56 the lost samples cancel the gain (ESS 8-10 at every λ ≤ 0.5), and
   λ = 1 collapses (16 accepted of 40,000).
3. Tilting also degrades |V_k|, as expected for weights aimed at b^-MD.
4. Accepted tilted boards cost more to weigh: 1.4-6.6x the backward-search
   nodes per accepted board.

Reading
-------
1. The late tilt is not a reliable improvement. The seed-1 gain at k = 56
   (ESS 40 against 8) reverses on seed 2 (7 against 22): at a few tens of
   effective samples the ESS itself varies several-fold between runs.
2. At k = 64 every configuration has ESS 3-9 for eta_k, and the tilt halves
   the yield (0.0045-0.0056 -> 0.0019-0.0020).
3. Single-run intervals are not trustworthy here: the λ = 1, last-16 run puts
   |V_56| 6.4 SE below the thesis value.
4. The low-MD tail of eta_k at k >= 56 is not fixed by any walk tilt tried
   (whole-walk λ = 0.25, 0.5, 1; last 8 or 16 steps, λ = 0.5, 1).
