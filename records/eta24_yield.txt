eta24 — sphere-walk yield and rejection cost (2026-09-16)
========================================================

Machine: M2 Pro, 12 threads (rayon default), on AC, NOT idle (Chrome, Defender,
load average ~6 before runs). Times are thread-time sums, so contention inflates
them; they are for choosing between levers that differ by several-fold, not for
percent-level A/Bs.

The instrument
--------------
`eta24 yield` (src/bin/eta24.rs) walks k steps from GOAL under the walker rule
(src/puzzle24/eta/walker.rs: legal moves minus reversal minus the MoveDfa W=11
prune mask, optionally with moribund pruning) and accepts the end board iff no
solution of length <= k-2 exists (src/puzzle24/eta/sphere.rs, reject_shorter).

  yield              accepted / attempts
  nodes, us/attempt  verification search cost, including rejected walks
  ms/accepted        thread time per accepted sample = us/attempt / yield

Clausecker's yields (ZIB Report 20-17, App. B) used an FSM matching segments up
to n = 22 (n = 24 at k = 63, 64) with moribund tracking.

Result 4 — how long a move-pruning rule must be to prevent rejected walks
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: would a longer MoveDfa window raise yield, and does the gain grow
smoothly with window length or in steps?

Instrument (a scratch program linked against the crate at commit 2adb010; not
committed). Walks come from the W = 11 walker with moribund pruning, seed 77;
a walk is rejected by reject_shorter with zpdb k6. For each rejected walk with
boards S_0..S_k, L_min is the smallest L such that some stretch [i, i+L] has
d(S_i, S_{i+L}) <= L - 2. d is found by IDA* with Manhattan distance to the
target board; L grows from 2 and stops at the first hit. A rule covering move
sequences up to length L_min removes the walk: a strictly non-shortest stretch
contains a dominated sequence ending within it. That coverage is a lower bound,
since a longer rule also removes equal-length duplicates.

Checks: no rejected walk has L_min <= 12 (the W = 11 rule covers sequences up
to 12 moves), and every shortest stretch saves exactly 2 moves.

Cumulative % of rejected walks with L_min <= R (R = longest sequence a rule
covers; window W covers R = W + 1):

Per-length share at k = 56 (% of rejected walks): L=13 3.3, 14 1.7, 15 18.9,
16 1.6, 17 12.1, 18 1.6, 19 9.9, 20 1.6, 21 7.8, 22 1.7, 23 6.0, 24 1.9, 25 4.3,
then decaying to 0.1-0.3 per length out to L = 56.

1. The gain comes in steps by parity. Each odd R adds a step (+19, +12, +10,
   +8, +6, +4 points at k = 56 for R = 15..25); each even R adds 1-2. The first
   large step is R = 15 (W = 14). Windows W = 11, 12, 13 cover R = 12, 13, 14,
   which reach only the two small steps (+3.3, +1.7), so comparing them
   understates what W >= 14 buys.
2. There is no single dominant relation family. Within the shortest stretch,
   the blank's bounding box is mostly 3x5 or 4x5 (5x5 from L = 21 at k = 28),
   not a compact 2x2 or 2x3 cycle.
3. The distribution stabilises from k ~ 40: coverage at k = 40, 48 and 56 agrees
   within ~2 points at every R.

Model check against the thesis. If removed walks' probability redistributes in
proportion over the remaining walks, the yield becomes y' = y / (1 - f(1 - y)),
with f = coverage at R. Using the thesis's R = 22 FSM and Result 1's
moribund-on yields:

The prediction is within 3 points everywhere and within 1.1% relative at
k >= 40. It sits slightly low at small k, as expected from coverage being a
lower bound.

-----------------------------------------------------------

(product-walk test). Build and verify, release, one thread

The yield ratios match Result 4's predictions (1.18x, 1.24x, 1.30x at k = 40,
48, 56 against measured 1.16x, 1.23x, 1.28x). The k = 64 ratio has wide error:
36 vs 50 accepted.

Time per accepted sample = check_every = 8 us/attempt / yield. Checkpoints do not
change verdicts, so both runs sample the same yield. Yields: the no-checkpoint
rows at k = 48, 56; at k = 64, both runs pooled (W=11 75/20000, W=14 95/20000).

   k      W=11       W=14     W=14 / W=11
  48    8.64 ms    7.93 ms       0.92
  56   93.8 ms    81.2 ms        0.87
  64    1.55 s     1.60 s        1.03

With prefix checkpoints the 1.2-1.4x yield gain buys at most ~13% time per
accepted sample, and nothing measurable at k = 64. Checkpoints already discard
doomed walks cheaply. The longer rule removes mostly those same walks and lets
more walks survive to the expensive late checkpoints: cost per attempt rises
1.13x (k = 48) to 1.30x (k = 64). What remains is the growth of the yield ratio
between the last checkpoint and k, which is small (1.28x at 56 -> 1.39x at 64).
Longer rules (17, 19 moves; Result 4 predicts 1.6x and ~2x yield at k = 56)
should be expected to buy proportionally little time by the same mechanism.

move_dfa_long.rs saves. Variants were measured in a scratch copy of the builder
(not committed), each window in its own process, M2 Pro, one thread unless
noted. Peak = max RSS / physical footprint. All variants were checked
equivalent to LongMoveDfa::build (product walk) through window 15, with equal
state counts through window 19.

  search phase     2.32M-board hash set (largest start blank) 68 MiB, frontier
