Parallel split — what makes a work unit expensive
=================================================

Measured 2026-08-12 on the exhaust-152 rung (Azure r156, 64 cores, --config
large so SPLIT_TARGET = 32768, cascade --clm2 --zpdb8). Companion to
r_flat_parallel_efficiency.txt, which measured granularity cost at +0.00s and
is NOT contradicted here: the split is fine-grained enough that an ideal
scheduler could not beat it. This record is about a different cost — what a
restart throws away — and about a signal the split ignores.

Occasion: threshold 152 finished 32,767 of 32,768 units and then ran for ~40
min on a single unit while 63 cores idled, and a spot eviction landed during
that window and discarded it.


Unit sizes span ten orders of magnitude
---------------------------------------
Node counts over 32,767 completed units at bound 152:

    min                1
    median       679,381,196
    p90        2,750,602,991
    p99        7,733,939,437
    p99.9     14,919,818,318
    max       33,808,570,978        = 50x the median, 29x the mean

The split cuts by BFS position, not by work. It pops from the front of a queue
and pushes children on the back until the frontier holds SPLIT_TARGET nodes
(engine.rs, `while frontier.len() < config.split_target()`). Nothing consults
how much work sits under a frontier node.


Slack predicts cost, and the split already has it
-------------------------------------------------
Every Unit carries `g` and `h`. With f = g + h and slack = bound - f, the
completed units sort cleanly (11,831 units, bound 152):

    slack   units   median nodes      mean nodes
        8    7312   1,170,303,781   1,666,700,703
        6    3299     259,569,791     395,380,851
        4    1014      70,568,776     130,446,543
        2     178      17,571,594      29,040,975
        0      28       4,733,385       5,055,548

About 4x per 2 plies of slack, 250x median across the range. A slack-0 unit is
nearly dead on arrival; a slack-8 unit has eight plies to burn.

The frontier is dominated by the expensive kind:

    f=144  slack=8   21232 units
    f=146  slack=6    8465 units
    f=148  slack=4    2539 units
    f=150  slack=2     451 units
    f=152  slack=0      81 units

All five largest completed units are g=13 h=131 (slack 8). The straggler that
held the machine at the end was g=14 h=130 — also slack 8, one ply deeper.


The straggler, reconstructed
----------------------------
The checkpoint stores `u <run_id> <bound> <idx> <fp> <nodes> <res>` — no board.
But the split is deterministic, so replaying it regenerates the identical
frontier, and ckpt_unit_fp hashes (board, g, dfa, last), which verifies the
reconstruction rather than merely indexing it.

Validation: 11,831 of 11,831 reconstructed fingerprints matched the recorded
ones exactly (100%).

Why this matters for the 154 rung
---------------------------------
Not throughput — eviction exposure. A spot eviction discards whatever all 64
workers have in flight. Observed eviction rate on this machine was 4 in 55 h
(~1 per 11 h, ~55 expected over a 31-day rung), so with mean unit time t the
expected loss is ~55 * t/2:

    SPLIT_TARGET   mean unit   worst unit   lost/evict   total   % of rung
          32768       1.47 h      42.7 h       44 min    40.5 h      5.4%
          65536       0.74 h      21.3 h       22 min    20.2 h      2.7%
         262144       0.18 h       5.3 h        6 min     5.1 h      0.7%
         524288       0.09 h       2.7 h        3 min     2.5 h      0.3%

(worst unit scales the 29x max/mean ratio measured at 152.)

At today's 32768 a single unit at 154 would be a ~43-hour single-threaded
subtree — near-certain to be evicted and restarted from zero, repeatedly.

Two options, untested:
  1. Raise SPLIT_TARGET. Measured sweep at 148 had 4096=595.1s 16384=576.6s
     32768=580.1s 65536=577.5s, i.e. 16K-65K tied within 0.6%. Nothing above
     65536 is measured. 262144 is 4x beyond the tested range.
  2. Split on slack instead of count: keep expanding frontier nodes with large
     slack, stop early on tight ones, producing units of comparable COST rather
     than comparable count. The signal is free — `h` is already in the Unit.

Both are changes to a soundness-critical path and must hold node identity
(115,436,814 at exhaust-144). SPLIT_TARGET is NOT part of ckpt_run_id, so
whatever is chosen must stay fixed for a whole rung or a resume silently
re-searches everything.

Also observed: every eviction costs a cold page cache on top of the lost units
— 49 GB of tables must fault back in before the machine returns to full rate.
Not quantified here. Argues for --hugepages on the next rung.


Which tier prunes the expensive units
-------------------------------------
Measured 2026-08-13 on the dev Mac (8 threads, --features search-census), to
answer the question the section above raises: the expensive units are the ones
the cascade fails to rule out, so WHICH tier goes blind on them?

Design. Two groups of 20 units drawn from the 32,768 of threshold 152, both
restricted to slack 8 so g+h is identical and any difference is attributable to
the board region rather than to search room:

    top 20      by node count, 16.2e9 .. 33.8e9 nodes on the real rung
    control 20  the median band of slack-8 units, ~1.13e9 nodes

Each re-searched at reduced slack 4 (--prove-at-least h+5), a complete exhaust
rather than a truncated sample, and ~16x cheaper than the real thing. The rates
are what matter; the absolute size does not.

Results (median, with min/max across the 20):

    metric                       top 20                control 20        ratio
    nodes                85,477,848 (59.1-145.8M)  3,909,612 (2.8-6.6M)  21.9x
    k8 consulted %nodes       70.00 (65.4-80.4)       68.65 (60.0-77.0)   1.02x
    k8 raised h %consults     24.25 (18.4-28.7)       14.70 ( 8.5-23.1)   1.65x
    k8 PRUNED %nodes          12.72 ( 9.8-15.7)        8.14 ( 4.5-10.6)   1.56x
    lm2 PRUNED %nodes          8.47 ( 1.3-13.4)        8.66 ( 1.6-20.0)   0.98x
    lm1l PRUNED %consults      9.68 ( 1.0-14.8)        8.12 ( 2.1-14.9)   1.19x

    k8 share of all pruning, k8/(k8+lm2):   top 59.4%   control 47.0%

**k8 carries the tail.** Its share of pruning rises from 47% to 59%, it raises
h on 1.65x as many consults, and it prunes 1.56x as much of the tree. lm2 is
flat to within 2%.

**But no tier goes blind, which was the hypothesis and it is wrong.** The
expensive units are not expensive because the cascade fails on them — k8 is
MORE effective there, not less. The 21.9x cost gap survives at matched slack,
so these boards are intrinsically large, and the cascade is working harder on
them and still losing ground.

That sharpens rather than answers the question. k8 is consulted on ~70% of
nodes in BOTH groups, so the same fraction survives cWD and reaches it. The
extra 22x therefore originates upstream of the tiers — in how many nodes cWD
lets through, or in the branching structure of that region — not in a tier
failing to fire. Untested.

Read-through for 154: nothing here argues for changing the cascade or its
consult order before the final run. k8 is doing the heavy lifting exactly where
it is needed.

Limits. Measured at slack 4, not the slack 8 these units actually ran at.
Medians over 20 units per group — enough to establish direction, not to fit a
model. Run on the dev Mac, where 49 GB of tables page against 34 GB of RAM, so
absolute throughput is not comparable with the VM; only the within-run rates
are used.

Reproducing
-----------
No tooling was kept. The frontier was dumped by temporarily patching
bounded_parallel to write every unit after `let units: Vec<Unit> = ...` and
exit — deliberately in the production code path rather than a reimplementation,
so the split could not diverge. Run with --checkpoint against a COPY of
runs/ckpt156 and --prove-at-least 153, which restores 144-150 and then splits
at 152. The fingerprint match is what proves the dump is faithful.
