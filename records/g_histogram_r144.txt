THE QUESTION. A radius-r goal-centered exact endgame database can prune an
expanded node only if that node is within r of the goal, i.e. g >= d* - r.
With optimal(R) = 156 now proved, coverage_ceiling(r) = #{expanded : g >= 156-r}
is an UPPER bound on what such a database could ever prune here. (The instrument
previously hardcoded d* = 152, the old published lower bound; it was corrected
to 156 before this run, which shifts every implied radius by 4.)

THE ANSWER: ZERO, for every radius up to 60. The deepest expanded node at
threshold 144 sits at g = 80, and r = 60 would need g >= 96. No goal-centered
endgame table of any buildable radius touches a single node of this proof.
The distribution is concentrated far from the goal — 99.98% of expanded nodes
have g <= 12, i.e. implied radius >= 144 — which is the same shape the cWD
depth histogram shows: an f-bounded search near a deep root spends its time
close to the root, not near the goal.

VERDICT: goal-ball endgame databases are dead for R, not on cost but on reach.
Nothing to tune — the intersection with the search is empty.
