Flat parallel driver — where the speedup goes
=============================================

Machine: Mac14,10 (M2 Pro), 8 P-cores in 2 clusters of 4 (16 MB L2 each),
4 E-cores, 34 GB. All runs below are exhaust-144 on R, node-identical at
422,379,806, single session.

*** ON BATTERY. Absolute ns/node here are NOT comparable with the AC baselines
*** in r_flat_engine_ab.txt (AC sequential was 13.31s / 31.5 ns/node; the
*** battery sequential in this session was 14.38s / 34.04 ns/node, -7.5%).
*** Every conclusion below rests on a RATIO measured within this session, or on
*** a frequency-independent counter (cache hit rate), so the battery state does
*** not affect them.

The instrument
--------------
`--features parallel-profile` (see Cargo.toml) times every work unit and prints,
per threshold:

    speedup / W  =  B  x  (c_1 / c_p)

with B = busy fraction = sum(unit wall time) / (W * parallel span), and
c_p = sum(unit wall time) / sum(nodes) = worker ns/node. The two factors have
unrelated fixes — B is the split policy and the scheduler, c_p is memory
contention, cache sizing and clock — so a lone speedup number cannot say which
to attack.

It also prints three makespan bounds. work/W is what a perfectly divisible
workload would reach; LPT is the best a good scheduler can do given that a unit
is INDIVISIBLE (a worker drawing a huge subtree runs it to completion); actual
is measured. So LPT - work/W is granularity cost from the split, and
actual - LPT is scheduling loss.

Validation of the identity, exhaust-144, W=8, per-unit caches (the original
code): B = 0.977, c_1 = 31.51, c_p = 39.70 -> 0.977 * 0.794 = 0.776. Measured
efficiency 13.31/2.14/8 = 0.777. Closes to 0.1%.

Result 1 — it is not the schedule, and it is not core heterogeneity
------------------------------------------------------------------
  busy fraction        99.5-100.0% at every W from 1 to 8
  granularity cost     +0.00s (LPT == work/W exactly)
  scheduling cost      +0.01s (0.5% of span), 0.09s (4.7%) only at W=12
  serial split phase   0.00s
  max unit             0.08s = 4.0% of a 2.12s span
  unit counts          382-735 across 8 threads (work-stealing balancing)
  per-thread ns/node   39.2-40.4, a +/-1.5% spread

The per-thread column is there because the busy fraction is BLIND to core
heterogeneity: a thread parked on an E-core still reads as busy while its nodes
cost 2-3x more. At W=8 no thread is on an E-core. At W=12 all twelve degrade
together (47.4-54.1 ns/node) rather than splitting into two groups, so the E-core
cost is not isolable to four threads — it is a whole-machine effect.

SPLIT_TARGET = 4096 is therefore already more than enough at this depth. Do not
spend runs tuning it against a 0.00s granularity cost.

Result 2 — ns/node vs W, same code path (so contention is isolated)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  W    ns/node   vs W=1   span     speedup   efficiency
  1    37.95      --      16.05s   1.00x      --
  2    38.94     +2.6%     8.23s   1.95x     97.5%
  4    39.83     +5.0%     4.22s   3.80x     95.1%
  8    39.80     +4.9%     2.11s   7.61x     95.1%
  12   49.84    +31.4%     1.84s   8.72x     (E-cores)

Multicore contention is +4.9% and SATURATES AT W=4 — identical at 4 and 8. So
shared-L2 / bandwidth / TLB-walker contention across the 4.4 GB table is close to
nil, and the whole-machine clock drop is inside this figure too.

Within its own code path the driver was already 95% efficient. The missing 20
points were entirely a W=1 defect: 37.95 ns/node against the sequential engine's
34.04 in the same session, with zero threads to contend with.

Result 3 — the defect: the probe cache was rebuilt once per WORK UNIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
`ProbeCache::with_bits()` and `Arena::new()` were constructed inside the `map`
closure, so both were discarded and re-zeroed 4096 times per threshold. A unit
averages ~103 K nodes; the cache is direct-mapped and warms over millions of
probes. It never warmed.

  config                      hit rate   misses    ns/node   setup %
  sequential, 256K            99.665%     1.42 M    34.04      --
  parallel W=1, 64K           95.147%    20.50 M    37.97     1.3%
  parallel W=1, 256K          95.495%    19.03 M    40.59     4.6%
  parallel W=1, 1M            95.613%    18.53 M    50.03    15.2%

14.5x the misses. Enlarging the cache 16x moved the hit rate 0.47 pp while
re-zeroing pushed setup from 1.3% to 15.2% of worker time — the signature of
COMPULSORY misses (first touch per unit), which no cache size can fix. This is
also why the earlier sequential 64K->256K A/B (-5.8%) did not transfer to the
parallel driver: it was measuring capacity misses, and these are not that.

Fix: hoist both to a `thread_local!`, one per rayon worker, reused across every
unit and across thresholds. Not `map_init` — that re-runs its initialiser once
per producer-split leaf, which is not once per thread.

  exhaust-144            W=1 ns/node   hit rate   setup   W=8 span   W=8 Mn/s
  per-unit    (before)      37.95      95.147%    1.3%     2.11s      200.4
  per-worker  (after)       34.25      98.914%    0.0%     1.91s      220.8

+10.2% throughput at W=8. W=1 is now within 0.6% of the sequential engine
(34.25 vs 34.04), so the penalty is closed rather than merely reduced.
Efficiency vs sequential at W=8: 14.38/1.91/8 = 94.1%.

Node identity held at every step: 422,379,806 at W = 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 and for
every cache size. Reuse is sound because `seed_subtree` rewrites arena depth 0 in
full and `run_iteration` only reads depths it has written — already why the
sequential engine reuses one arena across thresholds — and the cache is
transparent by construction.

Result 4 — per-worker cache size, W=8 (inconclusive at this depth)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  bits   entries   per thread   hit rate   misses    Mn/s
  16      64 K      2.1 MB      98.850%    4.86 M   218.48
  17     128 K      4.2 MB      99.351%    2.74 M   219.79
  18     256 K      8.4 MB      99.578%    1.78 M   221.00

Monotone but only +1.2% across a 16x size range, while misses fall 2.7x — i.e.
at exhaust-144 the surviving misses are cheap. Not decided here: 144 understates
memory pressure (the depth study in FINDINGS_R §8x showed L2_TLB_MISS_DATA per
node rising 52.7% from 144 to 146), so the sizing must be re-measured at 146.
Left at 16 bits, which is the L2-budget figure (4 threads/cluster x 2.1 MB =
8.4 MB of 16 MB).

================================================================================
PART 2 — exhaust-146, ON AC POWER (2026-07-29, same session throughout)
================================================================================

Caveat on absolutes: the battery was at 30% and CHARGING, which shares the SoC
power budget. Sequential exhaust-144 came in at 14.45s here against 13.31s in the
earlier AC session (-8.6%), and 14.38s on battery. So compare only WITHIN this
part; the ratios are sound, the absolute ns/node are not comparable to
r_flat_engine_ab.txt.

Every run below is `--prove-at-least 148`, which exhausts 144 then 146 and reports
both, so one run yields the depth comparison. Node counts identical everywhere:
422,379,806 at 144 and 17,767,093,830 at 146.

Baseline (sequential, no --parallel)
------------------------------------
  exhausts   time       Mn/s     ns/node   cache hit   misses/node
  144        14.45s     29.22    34.22     99.665%     0.0034
  146       675.43s     26.30    38.02     98.627%     0.0141

The 4.2x rise in misses/node is the depth penalty's mechanism, and it is why the
144 regime cannot decide any memory-shaped question: see §8x's TLB numbers from
the other side.

Worker count, 16-bit caches, exhaust-146
----------------------------------------
  W    ns/node   busy    sched   span      Mn/s     speedup vs seq
  4    43.73     98.2%   1.8%    197.85s    89.79    3.41x
  8    43.07     98.9%   1.1%     96.77s   183.61    6.98x
  10   48.33     93.3%   6.7%     92.08s   192.95    7.33x
  12   53.62     99.4%   0.6%     79.93s   222.28    8.45x

W=4's ns/node is 1.5% ABOVE W=8's, which is inside this session's drift — read it
as contention saturating by W=4 at depth, matching the 144 curve in Part 1, not as
W=8 being genuinely cheaper.

W=10 is the ragged point: 8 P-cores + 2 E-cores is the asymmetric case where
stealing cannot hide two threads running ~2x slow, so the busy fraction collapses
to 93.3%. At W=12 all four E-cores participate and it rebalances to 99.4%.

W=12 is +21.1% over W=8 and was DECLINED — it saturates the machine and leaves
nothing for interactive use. Available on demand via RAYON_NUM_THREADS=12. Note
rayon's own default pool on this box is 8, not 12.

Per-worker cache size, W=8, exhaust-146   *** DECIDED: 18 bits ***
------------------------------------------------------------------
  bits   per thread   hit rate   misses    ns/node   busy    span     Mn/s
  16     2.1 MB       96.026%    706.1 M   43.07     98.9%   96.77s   183.61
  17     4.2 MB       97.617%    423.4 M   42.14     98.8%   94.73s   187.55
  18     8.4 MB       98.592%    250.1 M   40.96     99.4%   91.52s   194.12
  19    16.8 MB       99.265%    130.6 M   40.20     97.1%   91.96s   193.20

+5.7% from 16 -> 18. This REFUTES the L2-budget reasoning in the old
WORKER_CACHE_BITS doc comment: 8 threads x 8.4 MB = 67 MB against 32 MB of total
L2, and it still wins. What the cache buys is not L2 residency, it is not probing
the 4.4 GB merged table — a miss is a SwissTable lookup that TLB-misses into
multi-GB territory and costs far more than an L2 miss on the cache itself. Minimise
misses; the L2 budget is not the binding constraint.

At 18 bits the eight per-worker caches take 250.1 M misses in aggregate against one
sequential 256 K cache's 249.7 M — parity — and contention falls from +13.3% to
+7.7%. That is the confirmation that the miss gap WAS most of the contention.

19 bits has the lower ns/node but ties on wall clock for 2x the memory (busy
fraction fell to 97.1%, scheduling rose to 2.9%). 18 is the knee.

Contrast with Part 1's identical sweep at exhaust-144: 16->18 was worth only +1.2%
there and I recorded it as inconclusive. That was correct — at 144 the misses are
4.2x rarer per node and land in cache rather than DRAM. Had the constant been set
from the 144 data, the 5.7% would have been left on the floor.

Final state, W=8, 18 bits
-------------------------
  exhausts   sequential            parallel W=8          speedup   efficiency
  144        14.45s / 29.22 Mn/s   1.90s / 221.8 Mn/s    7.61x     95.1%
  146       675.43s / 26.30 Mn/s  91.52s / 194.1 Mn/s    7.38x     92.2%

Residual at 146: 0.6% scheduling + 0.0% granularity + 7.2% per-node contention.
Identity check: B x c_1/c_p = 0.994 x (38.02/40.96) = 0.9226 vs a measured
675.43/(91.52 x 8) = 0.9226.

Granularity is EXACTLY 0.00s at both depths — LPT == work/W on a tree 43x larger
with the same 4096 units, largest unit 3.9% of span. SPLIT_TARGET is closed as a
lever; no runs were spent sweeping it.

vs the recursive engine (FINDINGS_R §8b, 8 threads, same heuristic stack):
  144:   3.31s / 128 Mn/s  ->  1.90s / 221.8 Mn/s   = 1.74x
  146: 157.80s / 115 Mn/s  -> 91.52s / 194.1 Mn/s   = 1.72x
This also passes the recursive engine's best throughput ever recorded in ANY
configuration (146 Mn/s, FINDINGS_HUNT.md:135, on the much cheaper WD heuristic
over a 55x larger tree).

Still open
----------
Splitting the clock term out of the 7.2% residual contention needs root:
`sudo powermetrics --samplers cpu_power` for P-cluster frequency at W=1 vs W=8, or
PMU CORE_ACTIVE_CYCLE per node (frequency-independent). Not run. Given contention
saturates by W=4 while clock would keep falling toward W=8, most of the 7.2% is
likely memory hierarchy rather than clock, but that is an inference, not a
measurement.

2-way associativity on the probe cache is not worth pursuing: at 18 bits the
direct-mapped hit rate is already 98.592% and the remaining 250 M misses match what
a warm sequential cache takes, so there is under 1% to recover.
